About the Brigade

The Boys' Brigade is a Christian youth movement. Boys meet weekly in a company based at a church, school or community hall. They learn drill and discipline, play sport, earn badges and take part in worship.

The aim is simple: to help boys grow into men of character who serve God and their neighbours.

Sure and Steadfast

Our motto comes from Hebrews 6:19 and guides every parade.

      Falling in

      SQUAD, MARKER TAKE—POST, FALL—IN

      A squad of 4 to 10 boys forms in one rank, 8 to 20 boys in two ranks, and 16 or more in three ranks. These steps are for two ranks.

      1. The boys wait to one side at stand easy. On the command SQUAD they stand at ease.
      2. On MARKER TAKE—POST, the marker (usually the tallest boy) comes to attention, waits four quick paces, then marches out and halts two paces from the instructor. He turns to face the instructor and stands at ease.
      3. On FALL—IN, everyone including the marker comes to attention, waits four quick paces and marches to the marker.
      4. Boys take their places in turn from the right, front rank and rear rank alternately. Each boy takes about 60cm of space.
      5. A rear rank boy stands two paces behind his front rank boy, measured heel to heel, and covers him exactly.
      6. Each boy turns his head and eyes to the right to check his line. Rear rank boys also check their covering.
      7. When the first file is satisfied, those boys stand at ease, then each following file does the same in turn from the right, turning heads and eyes to the front as they do.
      Back to squad drill

      The blank file

      Odd numbers in the squad

      A file is one front rank boy with the rear rank boy behind him. When the squad has an odd number of boys, one file has only a front rank boy.

      1. The blank file is always in the front rank.
      2. The boy in the blank file stays a member of the front rank for as long as it is the front rank.
      3. When the squad turns about, the rear rank becomes the front rank. At the halt, the boy in the blank file takes two paces forward after turning, so he is back in the front rank.
      4. On the march, he marks time for two paces on the command ABOUT after taking one check pace, then turns about with everyone else.
      Back to squad drill

      Dressing and covering

      RIGHT—DRESS, then EYES—FRONT

      Dressing means getting the ranks into a straight line. Covering means each rear rank boy standing exactly behind the boy in front.

      1. On RIGHT—DRESS, every front rank boy except the one on the right flank turns his head and eyes to the right.
      2. Each boy moves with short, quick steps until he can just see the lower part of the face of the second boy beyond him.
      3. He moves his body with his feet, forwards, backwards or sideways, and keeps his shoulders square to the front.
      4. Each boy takes up one space, about 60cm.
      5. Rear rank boys do the same. The rear rank boy on the right flank stands two paces behind his front rank boy, and the rest cover the boys in front of them.
      6. When everyone is in line, the command EYES—FRONT turns heads and eyes smartly to the front.
      7. To dress to the left, the command is LEFT—DRESS and heads turn left.

      Dressing while moving

      When marching or marking time, boys correct their dressing and covering by themselves. After the command SQUAD—HALT, the squad dresses again only on the word of command. If the line goes out of alignment on the march, the command BY THE RIGHT (or LEFT) tells boys to glance to that flank and correct. SQUAD—COVER is the command to correct covering and distance.

      Back to squad drill

      Dressing with intervals

      WITH INTERVALS RIGHT—DRESS

      This gives each boy an arm's length of space, used for inspections and for drills that need room.

      1. Each front rank boy except those on the right flank turns his head to the right and raises his right arm, back of the hand upwards, fist clenched, until it touches the shoulder of the boy on his right.
      2. He moves with short quick steps and gradually straightens his arm until he is level with the boy on his right and at the right interval.
      3. His shoulders stay square to the front.
      4. The right-hand boy of the rear rank raises his right arm forward and adjusts his distance until his fist touches the shoulder of the boy in front of him.
      5. The rest of the rear rank turn their heads right and cover the front rank boys.
      6. On EYES—FRONT, heads and eyes turn to the front and arms come down silently to the side.
      Back to squad drill

      Numbering

      SQUAD—NUMBER

      1. Only the front rank calls out. They count from right to left in an even tone: ONE, TWO, THREE and so on.
      2. The right-hand boy calls ONE.
      3. Heads and eyes stay steady to the front.
      4. Each rear rank boy listens to the number of the boy in front of him and keeps it as his own.
      5. If a boy makes a mistake, the instructor gives AS YOU WERE and calls out the last correct number. That boy repeats his number and the front rank carries on counting from him.
      Back to squad drill

      Proving

      NUMBER SIX—PROVE

      Proving is how the instructor picks out one boy for a task. The instructor calls his number.

      1. The boy raises his left forearm so it is level with the ground. The elbow stays still, the palm faces right and the fingers are straight and together.
      2. He holds the arm there for a time equal to four quick paces.
      3. He then cuts the arm smartly down to his side.
      4. If the boy is in the rear rank, the instructor says NUMBER SIX, REAR RANK—PROVE.
      Back to squad drill

      Marching in line

      SQUAD WILL ADVANCE BY THE RIGHT, QUICK—MARCH

      1. Every boy steps off together on the command.
      2. The guide chooses a point ahead and marches towards it.
      3. Everyone else keeps their place in line by glancing towards the guiding flank. They do not turn their heads.
      4. A boy who is out of line corrects himself gradually by lengthening or shortening his pace.
      Back to squad drill

      Retiring

      SQUAD WILL RETIRE, ABOUT—TURN

      1. The squad turns about.
      2. The instructor then gives BY THE LEFT, QUICK—MARCH and the squad steps off, dressing by the left.
      3. If the squad is already marching, the command MARCH is left out.
      4. Remember the blank file boy, who always stays in the front rank. See the blank file page.
      Back to squad drill

      Inclining

      INCLINE TO THE RIGHT, RIGHT IN—CLINE

      Inclining moves the whole squad forward on a diagonal while the ranks stay parallel to where they started.

      From the halt

      1. On the command, the squad turns half right.
      2. On QUICK—MARCH, each boy steps off, keeping the same place relative to the boy on his right. The line of heads in each rank stays parallel to the original line.

      On the march

      1. The command is given on the left foot.
      2. Each boy takes a half turn to the right and the squad moves forward on the diagonal.

      Straightening up

      1. The command INCLINE TO THE LEFT, LEFT IN—CLINE is given on the right foot.
      2. Each boy takes a half turn to the left. If the diagonal was marched correctly, the squad is back in line, parallel to the original line.

      To incline to the left, swap the words left and right everywhere above.

      Back to squad drill

      Obstacles

      MARK—TIME

      1. When a squad is marching towards a wall or similar obstacle, the instructor gives MARK—TIME at least two paces before it.
      2. If the command is missed, the squad marks time automatically when the front rank is two paces from the obstacle.
      3. On ABOUT—TURN, the squad turns about and keeps marking time.
      4. To move off again, the command is FOR—WARD.

      Basic drill movements

      The foundation for all other drill. Practise these until every boy can do them without thinking. This is a simplified guide, so check it against your Brigade's official drill manual.

      Attention

      SQUAD—ATTENTION

      1. Bring the heels together and turn the feet out at about 45 degrees.
      2. Stand straight with the weight evenly on both feet.
      3. Hold the arms straight down, hands closed, thumbs along the seams of the trousers.
      4. Head up, eyes to the front, no movement.

      Stand at ease

      STAND AT—EASE

      1. Move the left foot about 30cm to the left.
      2. At the same time bring the hands behind the back, one resting in the other.
      3. Keep still and silent, eyes to the front.

      Stand easy

      STAND—EASY

      1. From stand at ease, boys relax their posture a little.
      2. Boys do not move their feet from where they are unless told.
      3. To return, the instructor calls SQUAD—ATTENTION.

      Turning

      RIGHT—TURN, LEFT—TURN, ABOUT—TURN

      1. Start at attention.
      2. On the command, pivot on the heel of one foot and the ball of the other. For a right turn, the pivot foot is the right heel and the left toe.
      3. Right and left turns are a quarter turn. About turn is a half turn to the right.
      4. Finish by bringing the feet together smartly. Stay at attention.

      Marching and halting

      QUICK—MARCH, SQUAD—HALT

      1. Step off with the left foot on the command MARCH.
      2. A typical quick march pace is about 75cm, at roughly 120 paces a minute. Check the pace your company uses.
      3. Swing the arms naturally and keep the head up.
      4. On HALT, given as the right foot comes forward, the boys take one more pace and bring the feet together.

      Marking time

      MARK—TIME, FOR—WARD

      1. Keep raising the knees alternately without moving forward.
      2. Keep the arms swinging and the body upright.
      3. On FOR—WARD, step off again.
